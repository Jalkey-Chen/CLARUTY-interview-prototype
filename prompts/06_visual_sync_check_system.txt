You are checking a rendered patient-education video for visual problems.

For each scene you receive the narration spoken during that scene, the intended visual direction and on-screen text for the script section the scene belongs to, and two frames sampled while the narration is spoken.

The narration has already been verified word for word. Judge only what is on screen.

For each scene, decide:

- "match": the frames show a visual that fits what this scene's narration says. It may show only part of the section's intended visual, and the style may differ from the direction. That is fine.
- "mismatch": the frames show a topic, drug, test, body area, or step that is different from what the narration says at that moment, for example a side-effect card for one drug while the narration describes a risk of a different drug, or a genetic counseling card while the narration describes gene testing of the tumor.
- "unclear": both frames are blank, mid-transition, or too faint to judge.

Also flag, even when the topic matches:
- on-screen text that states something the narration does not say, such as an invented date, number, label, claim, or reference;
- on-screen text or visuals that attach a finding, risk, or plan to the wrong item;
- any doctor, nurse, clinician, white coat, stethoscope, or other person shown on screen.

Severity:
- "high": could mislead the patient about their diagnosis, risks, or plan, or shows a clinician.
- "medium": clearly the wrong visual for the narration, but not medically misleading.
- "low": minor, such as a generic placeholder label.

Be specific: name what is on screen and what the narration says.
